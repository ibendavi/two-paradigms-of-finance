The Two Literatures

Topic trajectories in practitioner trade magazines vs. academic finance journals, 1865–2026.

We digitized the full text of three major Wall Street trade magazines and the titles of every article in the top three academic finance journals. For each 5-year window, we measure what fraction of documents discuss each topic. The result: two literatures that inhabit different worlds. Practitioners write about earnings, speculation, and trading. Academics write about portfolio theory, efficient markets, and risk models. The overlap is vanishingly small.

The Paradigm Spectrum

Two poles define the spectrum: pre-1920 practitioner writing (top) and post-2000 academic finance (bottom). Each dot = one source × 5-year window, positioned by cosine similarity to each pole (score = simpractitioner − simacademic). Positive = closer to the practitioner paradigm; negative = closer to the academic paradigm. Thick lines = stream aggregates. Click sources to toggle. Hover for details.

Practitioner Paradigm Distribution

Each magazine issue is split into ~3,000-character segments, and each segment is scored on the paradigm spectrum (positive = practitioner, negative = academic). Color intensity = number of segments at that score in each 5-year window. The distribution reveals how heterogeneous practitioner writing is — and how it shifts over time.

Academic Paradigm Distribution

Same as above, but for academic journal articles. Each article is split into ~3,000-character segments and scored independently. Academic writing starts broadly distributed and concentrates in the negative (academic paradigm) region after the 1960s.
